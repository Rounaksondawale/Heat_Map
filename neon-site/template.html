Mission list

Three tools, one cheat menu

Which parts of a city are cooking, why, and how much green would it take to cool them down?

Mission 1

Where are the hotspots?

  • Average pixels into 600 m blocks
  • Flag blocks with Gi* z of 2.58 or more
  • Keep clusters at least 1 °C above their neighbours
models/hotspot_report.py
0.75 / 0.78Jaipur precision / recall against hotspots found on observed temperature (held-out city)
Mission 2

What is driving them?

  • Six crews: vegetation, built-up, albedo, water, terrain, land cover
  • Switch one off, re-predict, repeat
  • Each gets its exact Shapley share in °C
models/hotspot_report.py
0.89R² lost when vegetation is shuffled, the biggest drop of any group
Mission 3

What would cool it down?

  • Trees: non-water pixels with NDVI under 0.4 take the city's median tree values
  • Cool roofs: built-pixel albedo up by 0.15, capped at 0.6
  • The dose is the share converted when the gap closes
models/simulate_cooling.py
40 %trees close Jaipur's modelled 2.2 °C gap
Bonus level

Draw a box, anywhere

  • Draw a rectangle, 1 to 10 km a side
  • The app fetches Landsat, elevation, land cover and buildings
  • It runs the model and shows status, drivers and doses
dashboard/app.py
~4 minon a fresh area over a laptop connection. Instant the second time.

The garage

From satellite to cooling plan

Four stations. Each one hands its result to the next.

Satellites

Landsat 8/9, elevation, land cover, buildings. Up to 40 clearest scenes per city.

Features

NDVI, NDBI, MNDWI, albedo, terrain, neighbours. 51 channels per pixel.

U-Net

Predicts how much hotter or cooler each 30 m pixel is than its own city's average.

Tools

Gi* hotspots, Shapley drivers, cooling doses, and the live dashboard.
